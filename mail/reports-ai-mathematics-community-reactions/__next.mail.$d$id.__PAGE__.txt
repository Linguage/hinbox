<h2>摘要</h2>
<p>本文以中英对照形式收录 Proofs and Prompts 关于 AI 数学成果发布的59篇署名回应及读者评论，呈现数学共同体的兴奋、质疑与不安。讨论集中于证明的可靠性与可读性、人类理解的价值、青年研究者的培养、贡献归属与学术评价，以及 AI 企业与研究共同体的关系。各篇发言保留不同立场；文中涉及的数学成果、数据和争议主张，仍属于发言者当时的陈述，本文未作独立核实。</p>
<p><a id="introduction"></a></p>
<h2>导言</h2>
<p>当大量数学问题的解答被集中公布，研究者面对的不只是新证明，还有研究计划、职业路径与知识共享方式的变化。这份合集记录了不同数学家在这一时刻的直接反应：有人期待从新论证中发现联系，有人担心长期探索的空间受到挤压，也有人主张重新制定发表、评价和协作的规则。</p>
<p>阅读时，可以围绕两个贯穿全文的问题展开：数学进步如何转化为人类能够理解、解释和继续发展的知识？在工具能力快速变化时，怎样维护研究共同体的自主性，并支持年轻一代继续参与？以下保留原编者按、59位作者的回应和独立的读者评论区；每篇先英文、后中文，便于对照阅读。新增摘要与本导言为阅读导引，原作者的判断与争议则留在各自署名之下。</p>

<p>The results of OpenAI are almost unbelievable, Quasi-Riemann is a result that I thought was complete science fiction. Much can be said about the behavior of the AI labs, but given the existence of these models mathematics as a research discipline will have to undergo a massive transformation.</p>
<p><strong>中文 / Chinese translation</strong></p>
<p>OpenAI 的成果几乎令人难以置信。准黎曼（Quasi-Riemann）的结果，在我看来原本完全是科幻小说里的东西。AI 实验室的行事方式当然有许多值得讨论的地方，但既然这些模型已经存在，数学作为一门研究学科就必须经历巨大的转变。</p>

<h3>2. Roman Sauer</h3>
<p><strong>English / 英文原文</strong></p>
<p>A common piece of wisdom is that mathematicians only have a few tricks in their bag – which they skillfully apply again and again. I have such trick whose unreasonable effectiveness surprised me a few times: replacing a manifold by a certain measurable foliated object, a construction that comes from ergodic theory, and using it in unexpected contexts.</p>
<p>More recently, I relied on this trick in a paper with Sabine Braun on macroscopic scalar curvature, which was further extended by Hannah Alpert. This trick and these two papers now appear in OpenAI’s deeply creative solutions of problems 207 and 335 – problems so different, they could not be farther apart!</p>
<p>I am stunned.</p>
<p>What do I take away from this night? We cannot defensively narrow our focus to what we currently regard as human qualities in mathematics. This will force us into a diminishing role. Instead, we have to radically expand our view of what a mathematician is and does.</p>
<p><strong>中文 / Chinese translation</strong></p>
<p>人们常说，数学家的工具箱里只有少数几种招数，只不过他们善于反复巧妙地使用它们。我自己就有这么一种技巧，它出乎意料的有效性曾多次让我惊讶：用某种可测的叶状对象替代一个流形。这一构造来自遍历理论，我把它用在了意想不到的情境中。</p>
<p>最近，我和 Sabine Braun 在研究宏观标量曲率的论文中运用了这一技巧，Hannah Alpert 后来又拓展了相关工作。如今，这个技巧以及这两篇论文竟然出现在 OpenAI 对第207和335号问题极富创造性的解答中——两个问题几乎不可能相距更远了！</p>
<p>我震惊了。</p>
<p>经历这一夜，我有什么收获？我们不能采取防御姿态，把注意力局限在目前认定属于人类数学家独有的品质上。这只会迫使我们扮演越来越无足轻重的角色。相反，我们必须从根本上拓展对“数学家是什么、数学家做什么”的认识。</p>

<h3>3. Xiaolei Wu</h3>
<p><strong>English / 英文原文</strong></p>
<p>It is the last day of a week-long national holiday here in China. We had just taken a short family trip to a nearby coastal city. After waking up, I followed my usual routine and checked my phone. I was surprised to find many messages containing the same PDF file. Apparently, OpenAI had solved many open problems.</p>
<p>So I took a look at the group theory section. It looked unreal. These were almost all the major open problems in my field! How is it possible? So I sent a message asking where the file had come from. The answer was that OpenAI had just officially announced it.</p>
<p>I checked the group theory section again. Yes, these really were almost all of them. So Thompson’s group F is non-amenable. OK, I had tried it many times using ChatGPT, but I guess I didn’t have the newest model or enough tokens. Eilenberg–Ganea is false—not so surprising, perhaps, but how? OK, at least the Whitehead Conjecture has not fallen. And what? They had completely solved the Boone–Higman conjecture? And the ambient simple group could even be of type ($F_\infty$)…
I had expected this day to arrive, but maybe something like one year later.</p>
<p>After a while, I started to look at other sessions, starting from topology. So the Borel conjecture fails in dimension 4, the coarse Baum-Connes also fails…</p>
<p>The rest of the day felt long. More messages came in. My social media feeds were flooded with discussions about the list. Some people were still claiming that the problems solved in their fields were actually not so important. But I guess I had already passed that stage.</p>
<p><strong>中文 / Chinese translation</strong></p>
<p>今天是中国为期一周的国庆假期的最后一天。我们一家刚从附近一座海滨城市短途旅行回来。早上醒来后，我照例看手机，却惊讶地发现许多消息都附着同一个 PDF 文件。据说 OpenAI 解决了大量开放问题。</p>
<p>我打开群论部分，看起来简直不真实。这里几乎囊括了我所在领域所有重大的开放问题！这怎么可能？我发消息问文件从哪里来，得到的回答是 OpenAI 刚刚正式公布了它。</p>
<p>我又看了一遍群论部分。没错，几乎全在里面。Thompson 群 $F$ 是非可和的。好吧，我以前也多次试过用 ChatGPT 来解决这个问题，但或许我用的不是最新模型，或者 Token 不够。Eilenberg–Ganea 猜想是错的——也许不太意外，但怎么证明？好吧，至少 Whitehead 猜想还没有被攻克。等等，什么？他们竟然彻底解决了 Boone–Higman 猜想？而且包含原群的单群甚至可以是 $F_\infty$ 型……我预料这一天终究会到来，只是以为大约还要再过一年。</p>
<p>过了一阵，我开始查看其他部分，先从拓扑学看起。所以，Borel 猜想在四维不成立，粗 Baum–Connes 猜想也不成立……</p>
<p>接下来的一天显得格外漫长。更多消息不断传来，社交媒体信息流里全是围绕这份清单的讨论。有些人还在坚持说，自己领域被解决的问题其实没有那么重要。但我想，我已经过了那个阶段。</p>

<h3>4. Nicholas Williams</h3>
<p><strong>English / 英文原文</strong></p>
<p>We’ve certainly learnt a lot of new facts from this release. At the same time, it will take a while for us mathematicians to digest all of these papers. There’s no doubt that large language models have changed things enormously, but the end goal must always be human understanding. It feels important to point out that mathematicians do more than just solve open problems from the literature: we also have to grapple with the subject matter and think about what the good conjectures should be in the first place. But on the other hand, it does feel like the end of an era where the ability to solve problems was a distinguishing feature of being a mathematician. And this feels sad for those of us who like to solve problems.</p>
<p><strong>中文 / Chinese translation</strong></p>
<p>这次发布无疑让我们了解到许多新的数学事实。与此同时，我们数学家需要相当长的时间才能消化全部论文。毫无疑问，大语言模型带来了巨大变化，但最终目标必须始终是人类的理解。</p>
<p>必须指出，数学家的工作不只是解决文献中已有的开放问题；我们还需要深入探究数学对象本身，并思考什么才是值得提出的好猜想。不过，从另一方面看，以解决问题的能力作为数学家显著标志的时代似乎即将结束。对我们这些喜欢解题的人来说，这令人难过。</p>

<h3>5. Henry Bradford</h3>
<p><strong>English / 英文原文</strong></p>
<p>Today my chief feelings are disorientation and bewilderment. It’s as if the pace of events has outstripped us by such a margin, that it is impossible to know how one should respond, or even how to start finding out how to respond. My greatest worry is for the prospects for bringing through the next generation of mathematicians: we know how much time and effort it takes to refine one’s craft in our subject to the point where one can meaningfully contribute to the advancement of knowledge, and I’m anxious that the necessary incentives may no longer exist for bright young people to invest that effort. I am holding on to hope that a bright future for the practice of mathematics is possible, in which our understanding of the mathematical universe is enriched, rather than undermined, by the ubiquity of AI tools. To get there though, we need as a community to agree what a realistic pathway should look like for a young scholar to establish themselves in our subject.</p>
<p><strong>中文 / Chinese translation</strong></p>
<p>今天，我主要感到失去方向、茫然无措。事情发展的速度似乎已经远远超出了我们的应对能力，让人甚至不知道如何回应，更不知道该从何处着手思考如何回应。</p>
<p>我最担心的是下一代数学家的培养前景。我们知道，一个人要经过多少时间和努力，才能磨炼到足以为知识进步作出实质性贡献的程度。我担心，鼓励有才华的年轻人投入这些努力的必要激励可能不复存在。</p>
<p>我仍希望数学研究能够拥有光明的未来：普及的 AI 工具丰富我们对数学世界的理解，而不会削弱它。但要实现这一点，数学共同体需要共同确定，一位年轻学者今后究竟应该沿着怎样一条现实可行的道路，在这个学科中建立自己的职业生涯。</p>

<h3>6. Henry Wilton</h3>
<p><strong>English / 英文原文</strong></p>
<p>There are many problems on OpenAI’s list that I care a lot about: the Cannon conjecture, the non-residually-finite hyperbolic group, the Boone—Higman conjecture and at least ten others. I haven’t looked at any of them in detail yet, although a cursory glance suggests that the write-ups vary wildly in quality. It will take months of work to go through the proofs of all of them, to check if the proofs are correct and to try to digest them.</p>
<p>But I think it’s important to focus on the big picture. If OpenAI wanted to destroy the mathematical community, this would be a great way to go about it. Open problems are a resource that the mathematical community developed over decades or even centuries. The value they have is the value we have given them. They provide structure, a yardstick for progress and long-term goals. The advent of AI was always going to be a seismic shock, but this huge dump of papers is a tsunami that we have no time to prepare for. It’s clear that OpenAI will happily wash away our community’s structures to further their financial interests.</p>
<p>There is a long list of mathematical structures and norms that OpenAI seems to be intent on damaging. By refusing to name the authors of their papers, they imply that mathematics is no longer a human endeavour. They don’t submit their results to journals, which both implies that the norms of the mathematical community are defunct and makes it impossible for the community to digest their results. OpenAI haven’t shared fundamental information about how the problems are selected or the failure rates of their attempts. This information should be a necessary requirement for any serious scientific discussion of their tools. And of course there is the basic financial injustice that their hugely valuable models learned to reason by copying the very reasoning that our community made available for free through the Open Access movement.</p>
<p>I hope the mathematical community can adjust our practices and norms quickly enough to survive the shock of these developments, but I am fearful.</p>
<p><strong>中文 / Chinese translation</strong></p>
<p>OpenAI 的清单上有许多我极为关心的问题：Cannon 猜想、非剩余有限的双曲群、Boone–Higman 猜想，以及至少另外十个问题。我还没仔细阅读任何一篇，尽管匆匆一看就发现文章质量参差不齐。要逐一检查证明是否正确并尝试消化理解，恐怕需要数月。</p>
<p>但我认为更重要的是看清整体局面。如果 OpenAI 想要摧毁数学共同体，这会是个绝佳办法。开放问题是数学共同体花费数十年甚至数百年建立起来的资源。它们的价值来自我们赋予它们的意义，为研究提供结构、进展尺度和长期目标。AI 的到来注定是一场地震，但这次巨量倾倒论文像一场我们来不及准备的海啸。显然，OpenAI 乐于为了商业利益冲毁数学共同体的组织结构。</p>
<p>OpenAI 似乎打算损害的数学制度与规范可以列出长长一串。它们拒绝给论文列出作者，仿佛暗示数学不再是一项人类事业；不向期刊投稿，似乎认为学术共同体的规范已经失效，也令共同体无法按既有方式消化结果。OpenAI 也没有提供关于如何选题、尝试失败率等根本信息。任何严肃讨论其工具科学价值的工作都应要求这些数据。另外还存在最基本的经济不公：他们价值巨大的模型，是通过复制数学共同体在开放获取运动中免费分享的推理成果学会推理的。</p>
<p>我希望数学共同体能及时调整实践与规范，在这场冲击中生存下来，但我感到恐惧。</p>

<h3>7. Macarena Arenas</h3>
<p><strong>English / 英文原文</strong></p>
<p>Perhaps the AI companies could, if they wanted to, do something to benefit humankind and the planet we live on (ending world hunger, solving global warming, curing cancer…), but this is not it, and it is not being done for altruistic purposes.</p>
<p>Regarding the mathematics, some of it is unverified, most of it is badly written, and (probably) none of it is transparent in documenting how it was produced, who was involved, and what was its cost (in every sense). All of it is of course impressive. Some of it might come to benefit mathematics and mathematicians, but overall I think it is an example of a corporation trying to exert manipulative pressure on a field of knowledge, and I think it has been done irresponsibly. I hope that we as a community can find our way through this mess, but for the time being, it is a mess, and it causes more harm than good. It will distract a lot of people from working on their own ideas; it will disrupt many existing research agendas; it will create conflict in the mathematical community; it will encourage a lot of opportunistic, ill-informed use of these technologies (thus adding to the “slop” and creating more noise for us to navigate through); and it will discourage many, many people from doing mathematics.</p>
<p><strong>中文 / Chinese translation</strong></p>
<p>如果 AI 公司愿意，它们或许能够做些真正造福人类和地球的事——消除饥饿、解决全球变暖、治愈癌症等等。但这次行动显然不属于其中之一，也不是出于利他目的。</p>
<p>就数学而言，有些内容尚未验证，大部分写得很差，而且（恐怕）没有任何一篇透明记录了成果如何产生、谁参与其中、付出何种意义上的成本。这一切当然令人震撼，其中有些或许最终有益于数学和数学家。但总体来看，这是企业试图对一个知识领域施加操纵性压力的例子，我认为极不负责任。我希望共同体能穿过这场混乱，但眼下它造成的伤害多于益处：它使大量研究者无法专注自己的想法，打乱既有研究计划，制造共同体内部冲突，鼓励大量机会主义式、缺乏充分理解的技术使用（制造更多粗制内容和信息噪声），还会打击许许多多人继续做数学的意愿。</p>

<h3>8. Johannes Schmitt</h3>
<p><strong>English / 英文原文</strong></p>
<p>One thing to keep in mind about this drop of papers is that they were not created by OpenAI with the main goal of making progress in mathematical research, or even primarily for PR (there were some PR posts, but e.g. the final announcement was comparatively restrained). The main reason these papers exist is that OpenAI used unsolved math problems to evaluate their internal models for the purpose of improving their capabilities. They had to use hard open problems because these are the only ones still posing a challenge to frontier AI models. I believe that, from OpenAI’s point of view, the papers are essentially a byproduct of their internal development.</p>
<p>Thus the company could just have remained silent, or picked a few flashy results for another blog post. I think it is to their credit that instead they reached out to the math community for guidance on how to proceed, leading to the formation of the Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence. They followed some of the advice by this group, such as a timely release and (partial) Lean certificates, but not all of it, and in particular announced that they will continue to use open math problems for model development. In my view, the most pressing need of the math community right now is to have a central platform to discuss the results, start digesting the (by multiple accounts from colleagues, terribly written) papers, and report any mathematical mistakes or missing attribution. An OpenAI-controlled GitHub repository, with issues and pull requests disabled, cannot serve this purpose, and the ball is in OpenAI’s court to move the papers to a neutral repository and to back such a community effort with the promised funding for workshops and special programs.</p>
<p><strong>中文 / Chinese translation</strong></p>
<p>需要记住，这批论文并不是 OpenAI 主要为了推动数学研究进步而创作的，甚至主要目的也不是公关宣传（虽然有一些宣传文章，但最后的公告相对克制）。它们存在的主要原因是，OpenAI 使用未解决的数学问题来评估内部模型，以改进模型能力。他们必须选择困难的开放问题，因为只有这些问题仍能挑战前沿 AI 模型。我认为从 OpenAI 的角度看，论文基本只是内部开发的副产品。</p>
<p>因此公司本可以保持沉默，也可以只挑几个亮眼的成果再写一篇博客。但他们选择向数学共同体寻求处理建议，并由此促成数学与人工智能顾问小组（AGMAI）的成立，我认为这一点值得肯定。他们采纳了部分建议，例如及时发布、提供部分 Lean 证明证书，却没有全部采纳，尤其宣布会继续使用开放数学问题进行模型开发。</p>
